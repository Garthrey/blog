#### 26.1.26

i'm not sure if i've mentioned it before but i've been playing volleyball for about 7 years at this point (at varying levels) and am just generally always itching to play more with how much i love this sport. the social comp association near my old place was feeling out for people who'd want to cough up $200 for a weekend to head out to a regional mining down four hours out of the city to play some volleyball and get plastered, and considering the title of the blog- you bet your sweet cheeks i absolutely did that.

## day 0 - road trip/dinner
![driving](/_includes/img/drive.jpg)
so just in the best interest of keeping the stereotype alive, i don't drive so i had to ride shotgun with a guy i've played with for a bit but we're not too close since nobody else took up the offer to have little old me in their car (sad!). he swung around at 8am and we were off through a live action play-through of desert bus through the australian countryside for four hours! all things considered i was worried he was gonna be a lot more weird than he was, but we just ended up jamming to metal and nu metal jams punctuated with servo trips to get energy drinks every while. we got to the hotel super early, i was catching up on my assignment while the two people sharing with me just had a day of phone time since there's not a whole let else to do out here.

come dinner though, the mens (the one i was playing in) and the womens team got together for a cute little get-together and we just shot the shit that night and it was very nice to see them again since last i played with a good chunk of them i was a very awkward ~18-20 year old boy who struggled to hold a casual conversation because i barely felt human myself. now i'm a 25 year old girl with conversation skills who is barely considered human by most federal governments instead! in your face, liberals! but we had really nice chats and even the girls who i never had an opportunity to talk to that much were so lovely and everyone felt like a big family that's been friends for years which is always such an awesome vibe!

## day 1 - prelim rounds
![pub](/_includes/img/sunpub.jpg)
10am start with four games coming up. so fun fact about australia, it is fucking *hot* during late january and we actually had a forecasted heatwave of 46 degrees today which apparently the venue had made preparations to continue playing despite the shaking of the fist of my state's volleyball association. in reality it was awful, but seriously not as bad as you'd think for a 46 degree day since we had fans blasting and eskys with freezing water everywhere and mandatory drink breaks through the set. both our mens and womens team ended up undefeated (we on the mens side unfortunately had drew against the home team which was built to win, and we could only barely close out a draw from them in prelims, but still undefeated technically!) while the womens were absolutely stomping left, right and center.

### solidarity!
i had seen his name hovering around but there was a friend of a friend who i knew was transmasc on the womens team, unfortunately we had never really talked much before. though, fortunately, he remembered me and very quickly identified i looked Very Different and had a nice chat about where i stood about being trans in this regional mining town on a team of straight men and dads, and immediately connected over it which gave us a lot of opportunities to get closer to the women's team in general by proximity. one of the younger dads too, after seeing me change shirts and revealing my sports bra underneath (gasp) came up to me to ask about my gender, and i'm pretty blunt with these things and desensitized to the 'weirdness' of it so i was straight up and he took it very well and even made a few jabs about how loud i could scream on court to raise people's spirits which were very funny and very humanising in all honesty.

once we got back to accomodation after four games in the heat, one of the guys had invited everyone over to his cabin to have some gin and chat shit and i was shocked to hear people correctly gender this tiny little dude, and very quickly had a wake-up call that maybe i was a little -too- in my head about being a clocky bitch out here since everyone was so lovely and accepting of him from the get-go. but anyways, there was a shocking amount of spicy drama from our association back home and also from some of the clubs here we've played against in regional tournaments for a few years now, and just a nice little dynamic forming between us all.

8am semifinal game, unfortunately we were up drinking until nearly 2am so we unfortunately got a little swamped by the fresh-faced young'ns but all in good fun atleast! we proceeded to win up the other rounds pretty easy once we cleared up and secured second place across the comp snugly, there wasn't much to say about day 2 in all honesty since it was mostly just hanging around and trying to keep sweat off your back in the insane humidity where i ended up drinking about 4L of water and not pissing a single drop out (not even exaggerating here). 

### humidity
it fucking sucks. i will take 46 deg and dry over 35 and humid any day of the week. 






### hormones and sport
so in transitioning for two years now, i've decided that while accepted in the men's program it really does suck in such a social sport environment i can't get close to any of the guys on my team for the state season solely because of the weird gender-ness and the 19 year old straight boys not knowing What to do with me at all. so, shit scared, i applied for the women's team this year in tryouts since i knew there was a trans woman in the women's program on my club already and i knew a lot of the girls and got along well with them already, even though i love men's volleyball- i do just miss being close with my team and having that connection outside of training and even in training.

so that's where i'm at, and the entire comp i noticed just how different my body felt playing- my jump was like half of what it was, my usualy swing felt very odd with how much it was ripping up my shoulder, and i went from people being shocked at how tall + how high i can jump, to just people being shocked at my height. between games, i just wanted to do some research on the sentiment of trans women in volleyball and i saw a few organisations do the classic "trans men can play in mens, trans women cannot play in women's because of safety risks", as well as a constant re-visiting of this story of a 19 year old college athlete getting knocked out for 30 seconds because a trans woman hit a ball into her head, and she had a whole media run from it to talk about how trans women should be banned, etc. it's all sad shit, and looking through some of the (although limited) clear studies that biological advantages were actually extremely normalised between trans and cis women, and that trans women even had defecits in some areas- it is just very frustrating to see this form of bigotry take shape especially from people who've never played a sport in their lives but dictate the direction of sporting organisations. now i do agree that we need some studies on trans *athletes* vs compared to cis athletes of similar competition levels to see if anything pops up there, but it also raises a question of *why* are we doing all of this? especially in state competitions or social tournaments i do not understand the need for gender distinction over skill, or even height distinctions in the slightest. in my opinion the biggest difference is that women are usually less incentivised to pursue athletic hobbies when they're young, especially compared to boys and when you see beginners come to volleyball from either background it's very clear the biggest difference is primarily just coordination and familiarity with one's body more than anything.

but i would be lying if i said i still didn't have fears about trying out for the womens team, as someone who is 189cm and competitive for height with some high-level players i know i will get a lot more resistance for my transness versus the other trans girl who's around 5'8 or something and has been a lot more involved with her transition than myself who's okay being a bit clocky and boy-ish. i feel that my club will be totally supportive, but i do worry about *other* clubs getting up in arms about it and being catty/bitchy about it and that i might damage the credibility of other trans athletes solely for my height and performance at the men's level. but i'm confident that i have enough supportive people in the association that will advocate for me and hopefully be more openly supportive to trans athletes!
