## intro
![](/_includes/img/evoltbye.gif)

it's really funny how through all of school i kind of pushed myself into the nerd box since most of my friends were the academically studious types who would all play videogames together after school, and as a result kind of didn't give myself many opportunities to explore just how much i actually loved physical movement and sports. a lot of the time i even joke about how my love for volleyball is a 'glitch' compared to the rest of my interests and personality since i don't particularly care much for many other sports.

in 2019 i started playing in my state's state league at the lowest men's division and kind of fell in love with the intersection of the dedication, comraderie, and progression that accompanied the sport. training every week, constantly chipping away and getting better while also enabling your teammates to progress alongside you and eventually culminate on the court in results is an extremely satisfying process, and especially when you start noticing changes in your team's cohesion and playstyles. i moved clubs due to almost a full coup by our team against a,,, 'not ideal' coach to say the least and have been playing for said team ever since, including through my transition with this year marking my first year playing in the women's competition.

## transitioning as an athlete

trust me i hate using the term 'athlete' as much as you probably hate reading it but unfortunately my pea brain can't think of a more accurate descriptor atm.

### playing the men's game
so yeah, i played in the men's league from 2019 - 2025 from the lowest division and peaked at divison 1 (the third highest one) briefly before i had to reconstruct my acl twice and was out for two years rehabbing. until that point i always had this extremely odd sensation that i could not put my finger on, where i loved playing the sport with my team, and my team as a unit, but individually i just did not befriend any of my teammates and they kind of could smell a bit of faggotry on me and just said hello at a distance when it came to personal connection.

in my time playing it was always very funny that i would have these men on my team that just were rife with anger issues and ego, and in all honesty one of the reasons i even started playing division 1 i sincerely believe was as a personality hire since i just didn't have those same mental hurdles and was honestly there to have fun with my boys. at social events too, i just had zero interest in talking to 18-25 year old men about gambling and cars so ended up getting closer with a few people from the women's program instead thinking the entire time that damn it would be sick if i could play the sport i love, with people more like this. it was around the same time as when i tore my acl that i started consolidating gender thoughts, and actually started my medical transition around the same time (which was a kiss curse to be sure).

### trans athletes
i will put a disclaimer that i am by no means a medical professional nor have done any meaningfully extensive research, this was a cursory glance at a few different discourses.

of course the biggest (and most bullshit) argument against trans people (notably almost always trans women) in sports is that of safety due to difference in power/physical capabilities between male and female bodies. the research that i did in my paranoia of whether or not to try to play women's volleyball for the sake of my sanity kind of led to a few key interesting findings:

1. in normal populations, trans women displayed no measured muscular advantages to cis counterparts beyond predisposition to height etc. (which is still possible just more unlikely) and actually often cases had some defecits due to the whackiness of changing your body
2. the only advantage that trans women actually had is that some muscle gained while under the effects of higher testosterone could be retained through consistent training, but gaining new muscle would be identical to cis women
3. sporting organisations fucking hate trans women and also empirical evidence

## my transition
so with special note to point 2. which arguably is the only advantage trans women have over cis women as a population, i actually started my medical transition while having lost ~10kg of muscle due to atrophy following my surgery and inactivity. i had actually only started building up bulk as part of the program about a year into hrt, and then had to redo the surgery starting from scratch. so theoretically, i actually negated almost any advantages i got from being a trans athlete. atleast, that's the rationale that i try to tell myself but i find it so hard to truly distance myself from the rhetoric that's been shoved down mainstream media ad nauseum and still feel a deep-seated guilt and discomfort with being a trans woman in women's sports.
